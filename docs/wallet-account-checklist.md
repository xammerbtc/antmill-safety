# Wallet and account checklist

A short list you can reuse. None of these steps guarantee safety. Together, they remove the mistakes that show up in most theft reports.

## Wallet hygiene

- [ ] The seed phrase and private key exist only offline, in a place you control.
- [ ] You have never typed that phrase into a website, bot, form, or chat.
- [ ] A hardware wallet, or at least a dedicated device, holds any amount you cannot afford to lose.
- [ ] You use a separate, low-value wallet for trying new apps.
- [ ] You verify the full destination address before every send.
- [ ] You review token approvals and revoke unused ones.
- [ ] You do not import a wallet into a new app because a chat told you to “sync” or “validate” it.

## Telegram and social accounts

- [ ] Two-factor authentication is on, with a method you control.
- [ ] You do not click “Open App” or “Login and claim” buttons in unsolicited messages.
- [ ] You confirm official bot usernames from the project’s own channel or website, not from a reply in a comment thread.
- [ ] You ignore surprise DMs from “support”, “admin”, or “moderators”.
- [ ] Cloud backups of chats are not used as a place to store recovery phrases or screenshots of keys.

## Before you approve a signature

- [ ] You opened the app from a source you already trust, not from a DM.
- [ ] You can name, in one sentence, what the transaction or approval will do.
- [ ] The request is not framed as “claim now”, “verify your wallet”, or “unlock a refund”.
- [ ] If anything looks odd, you stop. There is no penalty for walking away.

## If something already feels wrong

1. Do not send more funds “to unlock” or “to pay a fee”.
2. Do not share a seed phrase to “reverse” a transaction. That is always a second scam.
3. If a phrase may have been exposed, move what you still control to a brand-new wallet, from a safe device, then retire the old one.
4. Warn people in the official community with facts, not panic, and report the impersonator account where the platform allows it.
